# Stakeholder Interview Log: LendAnIITian

**Project Title:** LendAnIITian (Peer-to-Peer Student Item Sharing Platform)  
**Team Name:** Team GGRK  
**Course:** CSC181  

---

## 1. Interview Guidelines & Core Question Bank

When interviewing fellow students, focus on **past behaviors and concrete stories** rather than hypothetical opinions.

### Core Questions
1. **[Borrower Story]** Walk me through the last time you needed an item for a short-term school activity (like performance clothes, lab gear, tools, or event props) that you didn't own. How did you try to get it, and how did that go?
2. **[Panic Buying / Wasted Money]** Have you ever ended up buying something brand new out of panic just for a one-time school use? Tell us what item it was and what happened.
3. **[Lender Story & Trust]** Think about things sitting in your room right now that you rarely use. Have you ever lent your stuff to a classmate or friend before? What was that experience like, and what made you worry (or feel safe) about handing it over?

- **Panic Buying / Wasted Allowance:**
  > *"Scientific calculator, because ABM man ko na strand sa high school tapos nagamit ra nako sya kay sa exam lang and wala na dayon. Actually, during lesson pwede raman mag phone for calculations pero sa exam kay bawal man phone and scical ra pwede, after ana kay wala na nako nagamit."*
- **Lending & Trust Concerns:**

#### Extracted Insights & Takeaways
1. **Discovery Deficit:** Students resort to physical *ukay-ukay* stores because social media pages (*MSU-IIT CATS*) lack organized search filters for item rentals.
2. **Single-Use Financial Waste:** Buying items like scientific calculators strictly for single exams wastes limited student allowance.
3. **Financial Collateral Requirement:** Lenders hesitate to lend valuable or sentimental items unless there is a rental fee or safety deposit acting as collateral to ensure borrower accountability.

### Interview #04: Christine

- **Participant:** Christine (3rd Year, BS METE Student)
- **Date & Time:** October 25, 2026 | 4:21 PM
- **Format / Location:** Online Chat
- **Interviewer:** Team GGRK Member

#### Key Responses & Story
- **Search Process (Borrowing Need):**
  > *"We had a workshop activity for our welding subject where we were required to have welding safety PPE and tools. Since our professor provided a list of the required gear and equipment, I went ahead and bought everything on it. However, when it came to the actual classes, most of the items weren't really used, and our professor ended up accepting alternatives anyway. Looking back, I realized I shouldn't have bought everything on the list, because several of the items I paid for weren't even used once.*
- **Panic Buying / Wasted Allowance:**
  > *"I ended up buying most of my welding PPE brand new for that class. Specifically, I bought earmuffs, steel-toe safety shoes, a welding apron, and safety goggles. In the end, none of those items were actually used, not even once. Adding to that, since high school, my parents have always preferred to buy brand-new books, manuals, and learning materials because that's what they prefer. However, most of the time I barely get to use these items, so they just end up getting stacked up or stored away.
"*
- **Lending & Trust Concerns:**
  > *"Most of the things I wasn't able to use were just left in storage. For my books, since I buy them brand new and rarely use them, my mom usually decides to lend them to a younger cousin or relative entering that same grade level so they still get put to good use. As for my notebooks and other items, especially my art materials, since I have a lot of them, my mom keeps them organized and stored away. Whenever I feel like using them in the future, she has them ready for me to take out. Since my books are usually in pristine condition, clean, with no markings or highlights, I sometimes feel a bit sad handing them over to relatives because I didn't get to maximize using them myself, and I worry their condition might deteriorate. However, I just remind myself that the books are theirs to use now, so it's fine. Ultimately, what makes me feel safe about lending my things is knowing that the person borrowing them is also good at taking care of their own belongings."*

#### Extracted Insights & Takeaways
1. **Trust is the real barrier, not availability:** There is already something that could be used in the storage, unused. Also, things are already going smooth with people in known circles (fam, relatives who mind their stuff), but with strangers/ casual DMs, we have ghosting/ forgotten returns/ what is it for. That needs to be replicated for a platform in terms of trust (maybe verification, purpose of borrow, and accountability etc)

2. **Overbuying by students:** because of uncertainty and habitual behavior. How things are mandatory on lists when actually profs usually accept alternatives. Also the family liking things brand new. So there are unused PPE, books, materials lying unused by students. The solution will be to offer a borrow first, buy later option and make it more clear on what is really needed.

3. **Out-of-town and new students are the most disadvantaged:** They don't have a network at the local campus, and so have noone to borrow from, and thus have to waste money which people with connections can avoid. These are the ones who'd benefit the most in terms of a structured system of borrowing.
